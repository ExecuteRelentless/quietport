What it is

A folder is the whole interface.

Quietport keeps one or more folders in sync between a small group of people. The person who runs it (the operator) creates a circle, adds members to it, and sends each of them a link. The link installs a small background program that puts a folder in their home directory and keeps it up to date. There is no sign-up form and no password to remember, because there is no account.

Every file is encrypted on the member's own computer before it goes anywhere, with a key that only the members of that circle hold. File names are encrypted too. The hub that relays the files is a plain server that stores blobs it cannot open, and it never receives a circle key. If someone walked off with the hub's disk they would have a pile of ciphertext and the sizes of the files.

Traffic between each member and the hub runs inside a WireGuard tunnel, so the encrypted files are wrapped a second time in transit. Members' computers cannot reach each other, only the hub.

Sync runs every 60 seconds when idle and within 5 seconds of a change. Files up to 10 GB resume after an interruption instead of starting over. When 2 people edit the same file, both copies are kept: the newer one stays in place and the older one moves into a hidden .qp-versions folder next to it, named with the time it was replaced, and is deleted after 30 days. The program shows a notification in exactly 3 situations: the disk is nearly full, a key was changed and the computer needs a fresh invite, or it has not been able to sync for 7 days. Otherwise it says nothing.

One example

A family with 5 circles.

The operator here is a member of every circle. The operator can read any circle they belong to and cannot read one they do not, because they never had that key.

CircleMembersMode
familyyou, your partner, Mom, Dad, Sarahboth ways
siblingsyou, Sarah, Benboth ways
photoseveryoneboth ways
documentsyou, your partnerboth ways
newsletteryou write, 12 others readsend only

Sarah is in family and siblings, so her computer has 2 folders under QPSync and no way to tell that documents exists. Ben has 1. Mom has 2 and, being in photos too, a third that fills up every time someone drops in a batch from a trip.

When Ben leaves the family circle, the operator runs qpctl offboard ben. His device is dropped from the network, the circle's key is replaced, the files on the hub are re-encrypted under the new key, and everyone still in the circle picks up the new key on their next check-in, within 5 minutes. What Ben already downloaded stays on Ben's computer. Nothing can take that back, and the program says so when you run it.

How an invite works

One link, one line, one folder.

The link works once and expires after 24 hours. Opened a second time, it shows a plain page that says to ask the person who sent it.

  1. The operator creates the invite. It names the person and the circles they get, and it carries the keys for those circles only. 
    qpctl invite create sarah --circles family,siblings
https://quietport.app/j/fbco4tqe5qh5wwfibe2oiuypfu
  2. Sarah opens the link on her Mac or PC. The page detects which one and gives her a single line to paste into Terminal or PowerShell. No installer to click through, and no administrator password, because nothing is installed outside her own user folder. 
    curl -fsSL https://quietport.app/j/fbco4tqe5qh5wwfibe2oiuypfu/mac | sh
  3. On the test Mac it took 59 seconds, then it printed Quietport is connected. and stopped talking. QPSync is pinned in her Finder sidebar with Family and Siblings inside, already filling.
Download

2 kinds of download.

Members do not need this page. Their invite link fetches the right build for their computer on its own. The downloads here are for someone who wants to run a Quietport of their own, and for members who would rather fetch the client by hand.

Operator kit

The hub and the qpctl command. Runs on a 2 vCPU, 4 GB Linux server (this one is an Oracle Cloud A1 with 12 GB and an 80 GB disk) with only ports 22 and 443 open to the internet. Setup is 2 scripts and about 15 minutes.

Client

The background program a member runs. It is useless without an invite link, which carries the network address and the circle keys. macOS 12 or later, Windows 10 or 11.

quietport-darwin-arm64.tar.gz quietport-darwin-amd64.tar.gz quietport-windows-amd64.zip

What is inside

Go binaries plus 2 bundled tools: rclone for encryption and transfer, and a userspace build of the Tailscale daemon for the tunnel. No kernel drivers, no system services. MIT licence.

SHA256SUMS.signed

Checksums are signed with the release key that clients also use to verify their own updates.

The rules it is built on

5 constraints, not preferences.

Everything above follows from these. A feature that breaks one of them does not get built.

C-1
Invite only. No sign-up, no discovery, no public access. Every member was added by an operator who knows them.
C-2
No administrator rights. Install and operation run as the current user. Nothing is written outside the user profile.
C-3
Invisible. After install, the only surface is a folder. No tray icon, no web page, no settings.
C-4
Zero-knowledge hub. The hub stores encrypted blobs and encrypted names. Possession of the hub yields nothing without a circle key.
C-5
Desktop only. Windows and macOS. Linux best effort. No phones, in this version.

Couple of things to know before you run one. The tunnel runs in user space because C-2 forbids a kernel driver, and that costs some speed on large transfers. And there is no key escrow: if the operator loses every copy of a circle's keys, that circle's files on the hub are gone. qpctl exports the keys, splits them 3 of 5 among people you trust, and can prove a backup still opens a real file.
